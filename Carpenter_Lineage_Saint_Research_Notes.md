## SAINT 1 — SAINT ARNULF OF METZ (research complete, 3 November 2026)

**Core facts [S/A]:**
- b. c. 582, Lay-Saint-Christophe near Nancy, Lorraine; family held vast domains between Moselle and Meuse.
- Educated at court of Theudebert II of Austrasia (595–612) under Gundulf of Provence (mayor of the palace, lifelong mentor).
- Distinguished as military commander (dux at the Schelde) and civil administrator; at one point six provinces under his care.
- m. c. 596 Dode/Doda (b. c. 584; paternal aunt of St Glodesind of Metz). Sons: Chlodulf (b. 605) and Ansegisel (b. c. 606).
- 613: with Pepin of Landen led the aristocratic opposition; Brunhilda overthrown, tortured, executed (broken on the wheel, dragged by wild horses); reunification under Clotaire II.
- c. 611/614: Clotaire offered him the vacant see of METZ; layman made bishop while continuing as king's steward; Doda took the veil at Trier.
- 624 (dark chapter): with Pepin of Landen encouraged Dagobert in the murder of Chrodoald the Agilolfing [recorded; hagiographers omit it].
- 625: sat in the Council of Reims; tutor and chief minister of young Dagobert I.
- 626/627: retired with friend Romaricus to the Vosges hermitage that became the Abbey of Remiremont.
- d. 16 August 640 at Remiremont, buried by Romaricus; translated ~641 to the Basilica of the Holy Apostles, Metz (717: Abbey of Saint-Arnould).
- The abbey became the CAROLINGIAN FAMILY TOMB: Hildegarde (Charlemagne's wife), his sisters, his son, Emperor Louis the Pious, Bishop Drogo all buried there; destroyed by Normans (9th c.), Charles V's siege 1552, Revolution broke the tombs; part of Louis the Pious's tomb survives in the Metz museum.

**Legends [marked as legend]:** (1) the Ring — thrown into the Moselle, returned in a fish (motif from Tobit/Polycarp — flagged); (2) the Fire — palace fire receded at his cross and word; (3) the Beer Mug — the undrainable mug at Champignuelles during the translation procession → patron of brewers (with the honest footnote: brewers' patronage often confused with Arnold of SOISSONS, a different saint); (4) the Doda head-shaving story.

**The parentage question [full]:**
- Vita Sancti Arnulfi (near-contemporary): "sufficiently elevated and noble parentage, very rich in worldly goods" — NO royal claim.
- c. 800 genealogy (Metz; modeled on the genealogy of Jesus): father Arnoald, son of Ansbertus + Blithilt/Blithilde, "daughter of Chlothar I" — unattested, a century late, dynasty-flattering → dismissed by modern historians as spurious.
- REAL EVIDENCE 1: will of Adalgisel Grimo, 30 December 634 (10th-c. Koblenz transcription; Levison edition 1932) — oldest early-medieval deed between Meuse and Rhine; "my aunt is interred" at the church of Saint-George, Amay; his clan = the Chrodoinides (names in -gisil; founded Wissembourg, Tholey, Beaulieu).
- REAL EVIDENCE 2: the sarcophagus of Saint Chrodoara, found 22 January 1977, 4:45 pm, 3 m below the choir of Saint-George, Amay; lid called "the greatest masterpiece of Merovingian sculpture north of the Alps"; carved as an abbess, staff, "friendly and comforting smile"; c. 730, made when her bones were exhumed — probably CHARLES MARTEL'S PROPAGANDA (Arnulf's own great-great-grandson glorifying his ancestress); 13th-c. silver reliquary (1235–1250) survives (Treasure of the French Community of Belgium); bones confirmed Frankish woman; her 13th-c. Vita falsely gave her Merovingian blood (father Childebert, brother Dagobert — weighed, rejected); scallop-shell pilgrim plaque (Santiago link).
- Chrodoara: b. ~560, possibly Swabia; m. Duke Bodegisel (Gregory of Tours's duke of Provence; praised by Venantius Fortunatus; murdered by a Carthage mob 588/589 returning from a Constantinople embassy); widowed, founded the chapel/abbey of Saint-George at Amay; d. before 634.
- SYNTHESIS: Bodegisel & Chrodoara carried as Arnulf's PROBABLE parents [B] (Vita Gundulphi + will + sarcophagus); Settipani concurs (Bodegisel line, not Arnoald) and argues the Roman-senatorial link, if any, runs through DODA (posits her as Arnoald's daughter); Kelley considered Settipani's Doda theory probable.
- The Ripuarian rungs (Mellobaude → Richemir → Theodemir → Clovis the Ripuarian → Childebert → Siegbert the Lame → Cloderic the Parricide → Munderic → Bodegisel I → Bodegisel II → Arnulf): rejected as "a genealogical fantasy to flatter the Carolingians... none of whom are attested in a near-contemporary source" [logged in §8.4].

**Cult:** began at Metz c. 642; no liturgical Mass before the mid-9th c.; the first = the DROGO SACRAMENTARY (Bishop Angilram of Metz, Charlemagne's archchaplain) — composed to protect the cult as Metz's prestige declined. Feast 18 July. Roman Martyrology: "At Metz in France, St Arnulf, a Bishop illustrious for holiness and the gift of miracles. He chose an eremitical life and ended his blessed career in peace." Iconography: pastoral staff or rake.

**The sons [NEW MATERIAL]:**
- SAINT CHLODULF OF METZ (605 – 8 June 696/697) — NEW ROSTER SAINT. Eldest son; married, son Aunulf; "despite a reputation for impiety in his youth" became Bishop of Metz in 657 (third successor to his father); governed 40 years and 15 days; decorated the cathedral of St. Stephen; d. ~90; buried in his father's church; body translated to the priory of Lay 959; venerated as SAINT CLOUD (the name behind St. Cloud, Minnesota and St. Cloud Hospital); at Nivelles remembered for closeness to his sister-in-law St Gertrude; feast 8 June; possibly wrote his father's biography; son Aunulf (d. before 714, no heirs) left property to cousin Pepin of Herstal; the Trudo deed of 662 (Abdella Trudo emancipates Chlodulf's son) has led scholars to suspect his family stood apart from the later palace coups → why his line was dropped from Carolingian genealogies. RELATION: brother of the direct ancestor Ansegisel — ≈42nd great-granduncle, collateral.
- Ansegisel (c. 606 – murdered by Gundewin before 679) — the direct ancestor; m. St Begga; venerated as a saint in some calendars.

**Relation: ≈43rd great-grandfather, through Ansegisel & Begga.**

**Sources:** Vita Sancti Arnulfi (Bollandists); Gregory of Tours; Fredegar; Liber Historiae Francorum; the will of Adalgisel Grimo (634); the 1977 Amay excavation reports; Settipani; Kelley (NEHGR 101); the Drogo Sacramentary scholarship; Wikipedia's synthesis (citing the above).

## SAINT 2 — SAINT PEPIN OF LANDEN (research complete, 3 November 2026)

**Core facts [S/A/B]:**
- b. c. 580/585 at LANDEN, Liège, Belgium; "Pepin the Elder / Pepin the Old." Father given as Carloman in the compiled trees [B/NA — tree grade; no near-contemporary source].
- d. 21 or 27 February 640 (older authorities allow 640 or 647) at Landen; buried at Landen; later TRANSLATED TO NIVELLES (date unknown); one tomb enshrines his body, his wife SAINT ITTA, and — the detail that survives — HIS NURSE.
- Translation legend [marked]: a great candle procession accompanied the relics from Landen to Nivelles, and though the wind was violent, not one taper was extinguished.
- Venerated at Landen and Nivelles from antiquity; NEVER formally canonized — "called sometimes Saint, sometimes the Blessed"; listed in some old martyrologies; feast 21 February; every Rogation procession his reliquary is still borne with those of St Itta and St Gertrude. Attributed the foundation of the Church of St Mary at Landen (subsists today).
- Contemporary praise: "a lover of peace and the constant defender of truth and justice"; good government, wise counsel; spread the faith; defended Christian towns from Slavic invaders; chose responsible men for vacant sees.

**The career [A — Fredegar and the early historians]:**
- 613: with Arnulf of Metz, led the Austrasian aristocratic opposition to Brunhilda; her overthrow and execution; reunification under Clotaire II, who appointed them MAYORS OF THE PALACE to rule Austrasia for his son Dagobert (623).
- 623–629: mayor under Dagobert I. THE REBUKE: Pepin rebuked Dagobert for his licentious life — and was DISCHARGED, retiring to his estates (one account says Aquitaine) for a decade.
- 639: Dagobert died; Pepin, appointed tutor of the three-year-old SIGEBERT III, came out of retirement, took the mayoralty again, and oversaw the royal treasury's division between Sigebert and his half-brother Clovis II (ruled by Nanthild in Neustria-Burgundy). Pepin and Cunibert, Bishop of Cologne (Arnulf's successor as chief counsellor), received the treasure at COMPIÈGNE and carried it back to Metz. Sigebert's share was amicably surrendered (Pepin's friendship with Aega, the Burgundian mayor). Not long after, Pepin died — the man who had held the kingdom together twice, dead within months of his return.
- Wikidata adds a daughter VIVENTIA and the trees add a Clotilda/Brunhild — [NA — flagged].

**The family — FOUR canonized children (two already in the roster, two NEW):**
1. **SAINT BEGGA (613/615 – 17 December 693)** — the direct ancestress (already in roster, ≈42nd great-grandmother). NEW DETAIL: made a PILGRIMAGE TO ROME before taking the veil; founded SEVEN CHURCHES at Andenne on the Meuse; abbess there; invoked for the cure of hernias and of infants' diseases; patroness of the BEGUINES since the 14th century (though NOT their foundress); buried in Saint Begga's Collegiate Church, Andenne; her vita written late 11th c.; the first nuns at Andenne came from Nivelles and brought IRISH monastic customs. ⚠ CONFLICT FLAGGED: one source carries her husband Ansegisel's death as "691 in a HUNTING ACCIDENT"; the main register (and the standard pedigrees) carries him MURDERED by Gundewin before 679. Both logged; the murder carries the stronger sourcing; the hunting variant flagged for the next pass.
2. **SAINT GERTRUDE OF NIVELLES (626 – 17 March 659)** — NEW ROSTER SAINT. Younger daughter of Pepin & Itta; Begga's sister → ≈43rd great-grandaunt, COLLATERAL. Refused marriage at ten — at a banquet before King Dagobert she rejected the son of the Duke of Austrasia: "she would marry neither him nor any other man; Christ alone would be her bridegroom." After Pepin's death Itta shaved her head into a monk's TONSURE to protect her from suitors and founded Nivelles (c. 639/640, on the advice of St AMAND of Maastricht); Gertrude became abbess at about twenty. Patronage: hospitality to Irish missionary monks — consulted ST FURSEY, gave land to ST FOILLAN (monastery of Fosses), aided ST ULTAN; in 656 she RESIGNED in favor of her niece SAINT WILFETRUDIS of Nivelles and spent her last years in Scripture and penance; mystic and visionary; died at THIRTY-THREE, "the age of Our Lord"; buried in a hair shirt and a pilgrim's veil. Folklore: the sea-monster legend (travellers drank the "Sinte Geerts Minne" / Gertrudenminne before journeys); patroness of travellers, gardeners, the recently dead (the dead were said to spend their first night under her care, the second under Michael the Archangel), and against mental illness; the mice — offerings of gold and silver mice left at her shrine in COLOGNE as late as 1822 (mice = souls in Purgatory); invoked against toothache and erysipelas. Feast 17 March. The original Nivelles abbey (double monastery: church of St Paul, oratory of the Virgin, church of St Peter with cemetery — she died in the portico of St Agatha's and was buried in St Peter's cemetery); the present Saint Gertrude Collegiate Church stands on the ruins; 13th-century gold-and-silver reliquary; the Berlin connection (Gertraudenbrücke sculpture, Sankt Gertrauden Hospital).
3. **GRIMOALD THE ELDER (616 – 656/657)** — the dark chapter; NOT a saint. Mayor of the palace 634–656; after Sigebert III's death (656) attempted the FIRST CAROLINGIAN COUP — put the Merovingian heir aside and had his own son crowned (CHILDEBERT THE ADOPTED); the scheme collapsed and Grimoald was executed. The family's near-miss at the throne, one generation before Pepin of Herstal. [B — the coup's details vary by source.]
4. **SAINT BAVO (Allowin) — NEW ROSTER SAINT.** Pepin's son; became a HERMIT; later canonized → ≈43rd great-granduncle, COLLATERAL. (Flag: not to be confused with the more famous St BAVO OF GHENT — same name, different cult centers; the confusion is logged.)

**Itta's siblings (orbit saints):** SAINT MODOALD, Bishop of Trier (Itta's brother); SAINT SEVERA, abbess (Itta's sister). [B/A]

**The parentage of Itta (flagged):** no direct record of her parents; the suggestion that she was daughter of Arnoald, Bishop of Metz (son of Ansbertus), belongs to the same late pedigree-complex as Arnulf's — Settipani's counter-theory (Doda = Arnoald's daughter) and the Itta-as-Arnoald's-daughter tradition are BOTH logged and weighed at their grade.

**Sources:** the Life contained in the ACTS OF SAINT GERTRUDE (the Vita Geretrudis); Fredegar; the early French historians; Alban Butler's Lives (the Landen/Nivelles cult details); FindAGrave/Wikidata for the burial (collegiate church crypt); the FMG Foundations study of Nivelles for the abbey archaeology.

**Relation: ≈43rd great-grandfather, through his daughter Begga.**

**ROSTER ADDITIONS from this dive (for the next write pass):** Saint Chlodulf of Metz (Cloud) — brother of Ansegisel; Saint Gertrude of Nivelles — sister of Begga; Saint Bavo (Allowin) — brother of Begga; Saint Wilfetrudis of Nivelles — Gertrude's niece (collateral); Saint Modoald of Trier and Saint Severa — Itta's siblings (orbit). The household of Pepin of Landen and Itta of Metz produced: two canonized parents, three canonized daughters/sons in the collateral line, a mayor who died in a failed coup, and the direct line of Charlemagne.

## SAINT 3 — SAINT BEGGA OF ANDENNE (research complete, 3 November 2026)

**Core facts [A/NA]:**
- b. 613/615 (some sources 610); d. 17 December 693, aged ~78, at Andenne on the Meuse; buried in SAINT BEGGA'S COLLEGIATE CHURCH, Andenne.
- Eldest daughter of Pepin of Landen and Itta of Metz; older sister of St Gertrude of Nivelles; m. Ansegisel, son of Arnulf of Metz; mother of Pepin of Herstal, Martin of Laon, and Clotilda of Heristal (the last's marriage to Theuderic III rides on the flagged Chaume/Settipani theory).
- The Roman Martyrology, verbatim: "At Ardenne, at the Seven Churches, St Begga Widow, sister of St Gertrude of Nivelles."
- Feast 17 December (her death day); some sources add 6 September.
- PATRONAGES: the town of Ardenne; against stuttering and of stutterers (a folk-etymology: her name resembles the French 'bègue' = stutterer); invoked for the cure of hernias and of infants' diseases; patroness (NOT foundress) of the BEGUINES — the Lier beguinage statue calls her "our foundress," but the scholarly view gives the movement's name to Lambert le Bègue.
- ICONOGRAPHY: as a queen (sometimes with three crowns); as abbess holding seven churches or a church with seven towers. PETER PAUL RUBENS painted the ancestral couple — "St Ansegisus and his wife St Begga."
- DISAMBIGUATION: NOT Saint Bega of Ireland (St Bees, Cumbria) — logged.

**The widowhood and the pilgrimage [A — Christian History Institute, the best source]:**
- Widowed "somewhere between the birth of her last child and 679" — Ansegisel's death: the two accounts now both logged with a third resolution offered by the CHI: "Some accounts say an enemy named Gundewin struck Ansegis but other accounts say he died in a hunting accident. Both may be true if the 'accident' was staged." The register carries: murdered by Gundewin [standard pedigrees], with the 691 hunting variant and the staged-accident reconciliation all flagged.
- c. 679, aged ~64: the PILGRIMAGE TO ROME, visiting the Seven Pilgrim Churches of the city.
- On her return: she took the veil and founded SEVEN CHURCHES at Andenne ON THE MEUSE — in direct imitation of the seven churches of Rome; in 691 she founded the monastery at one of them and became its FIRST ABBESS; the collegiate church of Andenne later grew from the seven churches (the monastery became a collegiate church of thirty-two canonesses of noble families with ten canons).
- The first nuns came from her sister Gertrude's abbey of Nivelles, bringing books and IRISH monastic practices — and, in keeping with the age's piety, a piece of St GERTRUDE'S BED (Gertrude had been dead thirty-three years), which the community held to work miraculous cures and which was adorned with gold and precious stones.
- THE FOUNTAIN OF SINTE BEGGA flows near Andenne — its water "miraculously hot in winter and cold in summer" [folklore, marked].
- Her VITA was written some FOUR HUNDRED YEARS after her death (late 11th c.); the Christian History Institute's verdict, logged: "even what we know is seriously muddled — a situation made worse when a seventeenth century biography embellished what little was known."

**Children flagged:** Pepin of Herstal's birth year varies by source (630 / 635 / 650 — all logged; d. 16 December 714 at Jupille); Clotilda–Theuderic III at the flagged grade.

**Sources:** the Roman Martyrology; the late-11th-c. Vita; F. Rousseau, "Le monastère mérovingien d'Andenne" (1977); the Christian History Institute's "It Happened Today"; the Genealogiae ducum Brabantiae (MGH SS XXV); Dunbar's Dictionary of Saintly Women; the AnaStpaul and vanosnabrugge saint-day compilations.

**Relation: ≈42nd great-grandmother — the marriage of Ansegisel and Begga is the single wedding from which both the Carolingian dynasty and this family descend.**

## SAINT 4 — SAINT ITTA OF METZ (research complete, 3 November 2026)

**Core facts [A]:**
- b. c. 592 (Metz per most; some trees say Landen); d. 8 May 652 at Nivelles, aged ~60; buried at the Abbey of Nivelles (present SAINT GERTRUDE'S COLLEGIATE CHURCH). Names: Ida, Itte, Iduberga, Ittaberga, Yduberga — the Old High German gloss on Iduberga: "the protecting seer."
- m. c. 613–615 Pepin of Landen; mother of Begga, Gertrude, Grimoald, Bavo. "By Begga, she is the grandmother of Pepin of Herstal and one of the matriarchs of the great Carolingian family."
- Feast 8 May (her death day; some calendars add 21 May). Relics at the Church of St Gertrude, Nivelles. Patroness of ITTEVILLE, France (the village founded on a farm she established); invoked against ERYSIPELAS (the skin disease) and TOOTHACHE. Iconography: a painting of her by EDWARD BURNE-JONES survives; her icon at Nivelles was the model for the church image.

**The founding of Nivelles [A — Wikipedia; the Dictionary of Saintly Women]:**
- After Pepin's death in 640, Itta and her daughter Gertrude withdrew from the capital for a religious life. The founding story, in Dunbar's words: "When Pepin died, Ida was inconsolable. Her son and older daughter were provided for, but she knew not what to do with herself and Gertrude, who was now a beautiful girl with a large estate. She consulted St. Amandus [Bishop of Maastricht], who advised her to build a double monastery at Nivelle, and there devote herself, her daughter, and her worldly goods to the service of God. She followed his advice."
- c. 647–649: the foundation — a Benedictine nunnery with a monastery under the abbess (a DOUBLE monastery). Itta may have appointed Gertrude as first abbess while living there herself AS A SIMPLE NUN, assisting the young abbess by her advice. The community doubled in size as monks came from France and overseas (England/Ireland); it became a double monastery when the nuns were joined by a group of IRISH monks who supported the operations. The growing influx of pilgrims eventually required the great Romanesque church — dedicated in 1046 in the presence of Wazo, Prince-Bishop of Liège, and Emperor Henry III.
- The tonsure detail (Dunbar): to prevent Gertrude from being kidnapped and forcibly married, Itta CUT HER DAUGHTER'S HAIR herself.

**The parentage [FULL FLAG RECORD — more honest than the roster's first pass]:**
- NO direct record of her parents exists. FamilySearch [A]: "Later genealogical traditions, particularly those emerging in Carolingian sources, associated her with the lineage of Arnulf of Metz, but there is no contemporary evidence confirming such a kinship."
- The traditional suggestion (Wikipedia [A]): a family of SENATORIAL status originating in AQUITAINE; daughter of Arnoald, Bishop of Metz, son of Ansbertus.
- CRITICAL SCHOLARLY FLAG: "The assertion in the Vita S. Modoaldi (1107) that the bishop of Trier Modoald was Iduberga's brother is NOW VIEWED AS A LATER FORGERY"; the Annals Laubienses (11th c.) and the Vita Modoaldi (12th c.) — the sources for the Modoald/Severa sibling relationship — "may not be reliable." So even the sibling-saints' connection to Itta is late and questioned.
- The live theories, all flagged: (1) daughter of Arnoald/Ansbertus (the traditional); (2) Kelley: Itta and Modoald children of ANSBERTUS, Bishop of Metz (NEHGR, April 1947); (3) daughter of GODINUS, mayor of the palace of Burgundy (Smith & Wace); (4) daughter of Grimoald of Aquitaine and Itta of Gascony (a French tradition); (5) the Depoin/Kelley/Settipani synthesis: Arnoald was likely a Carolingian ancestor through a DAUGHTER ITTA — Settipani argues the documented connection runs through Arnulf's wife DODA (posited as Arnoald's daughter), with Itta-as-Arnoald's-daughter undismissed; Kelley considered Settipani's Doda connection probable.
- The fantasy layer (logged, rejected): the pedigree making her granddaughter of King Theudebald via Arnoald I and Blithilde; the alternate tree making her daughter of "Arnoldus of Saxony and Dode of Heristal."

**Sources:** Wikipedia (Itta of Metz); Dunbar, Dictionary of Saintly Woman; Alban Butler's Lives; FamilySearch (94RF-G6N); the Vita Modoaldi (1107 — now questioned); the Annals Laubienses; Settipani, Les ancêtres de Charlemagne (1989); Weis, Ancestral Roots 190:9; NEHGR 101:112.

**Relation: ≈44th great-grandmother — mother of Saint Begga; the founding matriarch of Nivelles.**

## SAINT 5 — SAINT WALTHEOF OF CROWLAND (research complete, 3 November 2026)

**Core facts [S — the Scandia journal study, 'Waltheof: The Journey from Warrior to Saint'; A — DNB, 1911 Britannica, Orderic]:**
- b. c. 1046/1050; son of Earl SIWARD of Northumbria (the Dane of *Macbeth* fame) and Ælfflæd, granddaughter of EALDRED — son of UHTRED THE BOLD — of the Bamburgh dynasty. Earl of Huntingdon and Northampton from October 1065 (when Tostig Godwinson was banished); Earl of Northumbria 1072.
- The BLOOD FEUD he completed: Uhtred murdered by Thurbrand (1016) → Ealdred (Uhtred's son, Waltheof's grandfather) killed Thurbrand → CARL (Thurbrand's son) killed Ealdred → Waltheof avenged his great-grandfather and grandfather by massacring a number of Carl's sons. 'The chief stain on his memory' [recorded].
- m. 1070 JUDITH OF LENS — daughter of Lambert, Count of Lens, and ADELAIDE OF NORMANDY, sister of William the Conqueror. Three daughters: (1) MATILDA/MAUD — m. (1) Simon de Senlis/Saint-Liz (Earl of Northampton), (2) DAVID I, KING OF SCOTLAND (both her husbands were Waltheof's successors in title); (2) JUDITH/ALICE — m. RALPH DE TOSNY THE YOUNGER → the line that runs down to IDA DE TOSNY and the family's royal gateway; (3) a daughter said to have married Robert son of Richard (confusion flagged). NOTE: the register's chain runs through the Tosny daughter (Judith/Alice), not Matilda.
- Domesday records him ('Walleff'): his AULA at Hallam — now part of Sheffield.

**The fall, at full detail [S/A]:**
- 1075: at the wedding of Ralph de Gael (Earl of Norfolk) at Exning, enticed into the REVOLT OF THE EARLS (Norfolk + Hereford). He quickly repented; on LANFRANC's advice confessed to the King (in Normandy) and threw himself on his mercy. William treated it lightly — then arrested him.
- TRIAL AT CHRISTMAS 1075, at Westminster — HIS WIFE JUDITH A WITNESS. Imprisoned at Winchester; retried in May; condemned.
- Beheaded 31 MAY 1076 on ST GILES'S HILL near Winchester — the Worcester Chronicle: beheaded 'on the Feast of ST PETRONELLA.' Last of the Anglo-Saxon earls; the ONLY English aristocrat William ever formally executed; the act 'regarded as the turning point of William's reign' and believed connected to his later troubles. The English and Normans at court alike were horrified. LANFRANC insisted to his dying day that Waltheof was GUILTLESS. The dark question the compiled records raise — whether Judith's testimony was her uncle's reward — logged and flagged.
- The scaffold detail from Orderic: devout and warlike; had helped BUILD Crowland; expiated his faults during his year in prison; said the Lord's Prayer on the scaffold — its final petition uttered by his SEVERED HEAD.

**The cult [S — Scandia; A — DNB]:**
- Body hastily buried in unconsecrated ground at the execution site. A fortnight later ABBOT ULFCYTEL/ULFKETIL of CROWLAND — a foundation Waltheof had patronized ('good friend and brother of Crowland'; benefactor of Jarrow — he gave TYNEMOUTH) — at Judith's request and with the King's permission, removed the body and buried it in the chapter-house.
- Ulfcytel was DEPOSED ten years later — possibly BECAUSE he encouraged the reverence.
- 1092: his successor INGULF translated the body into the church (after a chapter-house fire). The coffin was opened: the corpse UNDECAYED, the severed head REJOINED to the trunk, only a red line marking the place of severance. Miracles began in great number — healing miracles, many involving the restoration of SIGHT.
- Norman monks derided the cult (a traitor, not a saint); the English venerated him as a martyr in the ilk of St Edmund King and Martyr; nationalistic miracles recorded, including a vengeance miracle on a Norman monk. The cult 'never attained more than limited local support' [Farmer] — a popular, not institutional, sainthood.
- Abbot GEOFFREY (d. 1124), though a Frenchman, would not allow a word spoken against the earl — and was rewarded with a vision of Waltheof in company with ST BARTHOLOMEW and ST GUTHLAC, the apostle and the hermit alternating remarks in a hexameter to the effect that WALTHEOF WAS NO LONGER HEADLESS, and though he had been an earl, was THEN A KING [Orderic — the single best sentence in the cult's record].
- The EPITAPH: the monks sent to the English-born ORDERIC VITALIS, who had visited Crowland, to compose it 'in heroic verse' — the Latin verse preserves Old English diction; possibly composed in Old English and translated (the *Valþjófsflokkr* tradition).
- Crowland's motive, stated by the scholarship: a saint's body draws pilgrims and donations; a traitor's does not — and Crowland held both the body and the family's largesse (the family's gifts to Sawtry, Elstow, St Albans). The abbey commissioned the Passio and the Miracula; the cult fused popular English devotion with the abbey's institutional interest, and even the Norman rulers found it useful — veneration of an English saint as evidence of assimilation.
- Sources named: the Anglo-Saxon Chronicle (D and E), the Easter Table Chronicle, ORDERIC VITALIS, the PASSIO WALTHDEVI (MS Douai 852), the MIRACULA S. WALDEVI, GEFFREI GAIMAR.

**The grandson-saint [A — DNB]: WALTHEOF OF MELROSE (d. 1159)** — second son of Simon de Senlis by Matilda; the FORMALLY canonized Waltheof. Canon regular at Nostal; prior of Kirkham (his monks invoked ecclesiastical censure to stop him leaving); Cistercian at Wardon and Rievaulx; elected ABBOT OF MELROSE 1148; mild to others, severe to himself; would not allow his high birth to be mentioned; visions kept concealed until his death; influenced his brother to found Sawtrey, his half-brother Henry to found Holm Cultram, his stepfather David to found Kinloss, his nephew Malcolm to found Cupar; elected BISHOP OF GLASGOW just before his death — and refused the honor. RELATION: grandson of the 30th great-grandfather, via the daughter Matilda — collateral (the direct line runs through the Tosny daughter). NEW ROSTER CANDIDATE.

**Judith's afterlife:** Domesday's 'Judith the Countess' — estates in many counties, most apparently gifts from the King; held Huntingdon in dower; founded the Benedictine nunnery of ELSTOW, near Bedford.

**Core facts [S — Britannica; A — Wikipedia, the Henry Project, Ruotger's Vita (MGH SS 4:252–275)]:**

- Chancellor from 940 (the chancery reformed, 'a training ground for capable administrators'); archchaplain 951; accompanied Otto to Italy.

Otto appointed Bruno: most of Lotharingia lay inside the Cologne archdiocese. The rebellion collapsed in 954; Conrad deposed; Bruno made DUKE OF LOTHARINGIA — 'ARCHDUKE,' his biographer Ruotger called him — the most powerful man after Otto in all Europe. 959: the duchy split into Lower and Upper Lotharingia (Godfrey, Frederick as margraves).

## SAINT 1 — SAINT ARNULF OF METZ: EXPANSION PASS 2 (3 November 2026 — 613 and Remiremont)

**THE 613 REVOLUTION, sharpened [A — Fredegar, Chronicle IV; the Liber Historiae Francorum]:**
- The run-up: the 610–612 civil war between Theuderic II of Burgundy and Theudebert II of Austrasia; the battles of TOUL and ZÜLPICH (612) so destructive that Fredegar's famous line runs 'the dying could not fall to the ground, but were propped up in an erect position between the heaps of slain.' Theuderic won, executed his captive brother Theudebert, and had Theudebert's infant son Merovech dashed to pieces against a rock. Theuderic died of dysentery at METZ in 613, aged twenty-six.
- Brunhilda's third regency: ruling for her great-grandson SIGEBERT II, she purged the aristocracy; the mayors WARNACHAR, RADO, PEPIN OF LANDEN and ARNULF fled to Chlothar II of Neustria. FREDEgAR CALLS ARNULF AND PEPIN 'THE TWO MOST POWERST BARONS OF AUSTRASIA' — the phrase, sourced — and they made their agreement with Chlothar at ANDERNACH.
- Battle of the Aisne (613): more dukes defected mid-battle; Brunhilda and Sigebert II fled toward Orbe in Switzerland seeking allies; captured. Sigebert II and his brothers were killed (one Merovech escaped to a monastery).
- THE EXECUTION, in the Liber Historiae Francorum's own words: 'Then the army of the Franks and Burgundians joined into one, all shouted together that death would be most fitting for the very wicked Brunhilda. Then King Clotaire ordered that she be lifted onto a CAMEL and led through the entire army. Then she was tied to the feet of WILD HORSES and torn apart limb from limb. Finally she died. Her final grave was the fire. Her bones were burnt.' Chlothar accused her of the deaths of TEN royal family members (Sigebert I, Chilperic I, Theudebert II, Theuderic II, Sigebert II, the Merovechs, Corbo, Theudebert's sons) and many churchmen. *(Correction to the first pass: the camel-parade and horses-and-fire version is Fredegar/LHF; the 'broken on the wheel' variant is a later tradition — both logged, LHF preferred.)* Legend: dragged down the Roman road La Chaussée Brunehaut at Abbeville [marked].
- The reward asymmetry, documented: Rado was confirmed mayor in Austrasia, Warnachar in Burgundy — PEPIN RECEIVED NOTHING UNTIL 623, when Dagobert was made king of Austrasia; ARNULF was appointed the boy king's adviser. Ten years of patience between the revolution and the reward.
- Pepin's 629 retirement: Wikipedia carries 'for reasons UNKNOWN, he retired (or was retired)'; the Butler tradition carries the rebuke of Dagobert's licentious life. BOTH logged — the honest answer is the sources disagree on why.

**PEPIN'S FATHER — UPGRADED [A — Fredegar]:** 'Pepin's father is named CARLOMAN by the Chronicle of Fredegar, the chief source for his life.' The earlier staging note ('B/NA tree grade') is corrected: Fredegar names Carloman. **REMIREMONT, fully [S — Britannica; A — Wikipedia, the Catholic Encyclopedia]:**
- Founded c. 620 by SAINT ROMARIC (580–653) — a lord at Clotaire II's court, A COMPANION OF ST COLUMBAN AT LUXEUIL, converted by AMATUS, a monk of Luxeuil — the Irish thread confirmed end to end: Columbanus → Luxeuil → Amatus → Romaric → Remiremont → Arnulf. (Hagiographic detail, flagged: Romaric's parents were killed by Brunhilda — his founding as vengeance-turned-prayer.)
- Saint-Mont (Mount Haberd), above the Moselle valley: a DOUBLE MONASTERY under the Rule of ST COLUMBANUS, practicing the LAUS PERENNIS — the perpetual chant, the Office sung without ceasing by alternating choirs. THE VITA SANCTI ARNULFI'S IRISH INFLUENCE AND THE REMIREMONT COMPOSITION SITE (the Brepols finding) now cohere with the house's actual Columbanian filiation.
- 'Around 640, Bishop Arnulf of Metz, progenitor of the Arnulfing and Carolingian dynasty, died near HABENDUM, and was BURIED IN THE MONASTERY until his remains were later translated to Metz' [Remiremont Abbey's own record] — the burial detail fixed.
- The later history, one paragraph of wonder: 910, the Hungarian invasion drove the nuns down to the valley town; the LADIES OF REMIREMONT became the most exclusive chapter in Europe — the abbess a PRINCESS OF THE HOLY ROMAN EMPIRE (1290), every postulant required to show SIXTEEN QUARTERS of nobility without misalliance; Emperor Rudolf married Princess Elizabeth there (1284); the War of the Escutcheons (1564) between the duke of Lorraine and the abbess; Catherine of Lorraine's failed reform; suppressed in the Revolution. The abbey church keeps its 11th-century crypt.

## SAINT 1 — SAINT ARNULF OF METZ: THE VATICAN PASS (3 November 2026 — "maybe in the Vatican archives online?")

**THE SECRET ARCHIVE, honestly closed:** the Vatican Apostolic Archive holds NOTHING from Arnulf's era: its own documentation gives its holdings as 'about twelve centuries (8th to 20th)' — the oldest document dates from the end of the 8th century; complete papal letter registers begin with INNOCENT III (1198). Even the 2012 Lux in arcana exhibition (100 documents) opened with the 8th century. The Catholic Encyclopedia's regesta article confirms: 'Nearly all the volumes of the papal regesta up to the end of the twelfth century have disappeared... From the centuries previous to the pontificate of Innocent III there remain only fragments' — Gregory I's ~850 letters (590–604), John VIII fragments, scattered letters of others. For 604–872, no register survives.

**BUT THE REGESTA PASS FOUND A THREAD [S — Jankowiak, 'Misdated popes' (Oxford/BECh 2022), n. 64]:** 'More interesting is the privilege that a pope John issued at the request of a king Chlodwig for a Merovingian monastery, perhaps Luxeuil. Such a combination of names is only possible for POPE JOHN IV and CLOVIS (CHLODWIG) II, king of Neustria since January 639. The original is lost, but was "recycled" in five spurious privileges for Frankish monasteries.' The five: St Cross of Meaux, Rebais, a monastery of Mary, Columba and Agatha, Luxeuil — and **REMIREMONT: Regesta 3304–5 and 3311–13; Pardessus, Diplomata 2, 65–69 and 71–80, nos 298–9 and 301–4.** A papal privilege of John IV (640–642), requested by Clovis II, touching REMIREMONT — Arnulf's own hermitage monastery — at the very moment of Arnulf's death and translation. EWIG's study (1974, 'Bemerkungen zu zwei merowingischen Bischofsprivilegien und einem Papstprivileg des 7. Jahrhunderts für merowingische Klöster') analyzed the recycled corpus: GENUINE CORE vs SPURIOUS COPIES — the register carries both halves.

**THE CHRONOLOGY CORRECTION [S — Jankowiak]:** the dates of the popes 619–649 are ONE YEAR TOO LATE in the standard chronology (a double error: a year inserted between Deusdedit and Boniface V, and the first year of Martin removed) — all pontificates 619–649 move one year back: BONIFACE V (619–625), HONORIUS I (d. 12 October 637), SEVERINUS (May–August 640), JOHN IV (640–642). The dossier of Honorius's last year re-dated accordingly.

**THE PAPAL WORLD OF ARNULF'S BISHOPRIC [A — Catholic Encyclopedia, Honorius I]:** Honorius I (625–638) — the Monothelite letters to Sergius (later condemned at Constantinople III, 680); the English missions (St Birinus to the West Saxons; the pallium to Paulinus of York 634; the letter to King Edwin; the Irish Easter letter 630 → the Council of Magh Lene 630/631 and southern Ireland's adoption of the Roman use 633); the restoration of the Roman churches. NO document names Arnulf — the pope of Arnulf's era was fighting the Monothelite war and running the English missions, and the Frankish mayors were below his register.

**THE COLOGNE DOOR [B — Repertorium Saracenorum, saraceni.uni-koeln.de]:** the Vita sancti Arnulfi entry in the Cologne early-medieval research wiki, citing Nason's Sacris Erudiri 54 (2015) 171–200 — the Vita's scholarly anchor page.

**VERDICT, stated once:** the Vatican's Secret Archive is closed to this family by a five-century gap; but a papal privilege for Remiremont, requested by Clovis II, issued in the months around Arnulf's death, survives as a reconstructed core inside five corrupted copies — the closest Rome ever came to touching the oldest graves. The next-read documents: Ewig 1974 (the privilege's analysis) and Pardessus nos 298–9, 301–4 (the texts).

## SAINT 1 — SAINT ARNULF OF METZ: THE VITA PASS (3 November 2026 — the seventh-century text itself)

**THE VITA'S OWN WORDS [S — Krusch, MGH SS rer. Merov. 2, pp. 432–437, quoted via the Šenk (Charles University) study and the ancientlibrary.net Latin text]:**
- c. 1: "BEATUS IGITUR ARNULFUS EPISCOPUS PROSAPIE GENITUS FRANCORUM, ALTUS SATIS ET NOBILIS PARENTIBUS ATQUE OPPULENTISSIMUS IN REBUS SAECULI FUIT" — 'Blessed Bishop Arnulf was born of FRANKISH STOCK, of parents sufficiently high and noble and MOST RICH in worldly goods.'
- The election: "FORTE FUIT, UT URBS METENSIUM PRAESULE INDIGERET. TUNC UNA VOX POPULORUM ARNULFUM DOMESTICUM ATQUE CONSILIARIUM REGIS DIGNUM ESSE EPISCOPUM ADCLAMAVIT" — 'It happened that the city of Metz lacked a bishop. Then with ONE VOICE the people acclaimed Arnulf, the king's household officer and counselor, worthy to be bishop.' (The people, not just the king — an acclamation Vita.)
- THE THURINGIAN JOURNEY: the Vita carries Arnulf on a journey to THURINGIA, with 'Noddo' (Duke Notto) — V. Schimpff's study (Terra praehistorica, 2007, pp. 411–415) treats the Frankish-Thuringian relations of the first third of the 7th century on the basis of this passage [NEW EPISODE — flagged for the full read].
- THE TRANSLATION MIRACLES, from the Latin text: (1) Bishop GOERIC of Metz, hearing of the death, came with two other bishops and a great crowd of clergy and laymen, and bore the body back with great reverence; at a river whose banks were slippery, the rear bearers FELL — and, 'as I believe, angels were at hand,' for the front bearers went on unhindered until the fallen rose visibly before all and resumed their place under the bier; (2) the food miracle at Duke Notto's: 'Quia parum quid in promptu habemus, sanctus Arnolfus nocte hac provideat nobis' — so great an abundance of food and drink grew that, all being satisfied, HALF remained over for the morrow; (3) the people ran to meet the body 'with crosses and candles, with great joy and wonder, receiving their shepherd, whom they had long ago lost by God's judgment as a fugitive, now reigning in heaven'; (4) the burial: 'sacrum corpus illius in basilica sanctorum apostolorum cum magna reverentia recondunt. QUO SEPULTO, QUID APUD CHRISTUM IN EREMO MERCATUS FUERIT CIVIBUS SUIS MIRACULIS DEMONSTRAVIT' — 'they laid his sacred body in the basilica of the Holy Apostles with great reverence; and once buried, BY WHAT HE HAD PURCHASED FOR HIMSELF WITH CHRIST IN THE DESERT he showed to his fellow citizens by his miracles.' The Vita's own best sentence.
- THE DAGOBERT PALACE EPISODE: a leper cried out to Arnulf in the palace court asking food or clothing; Arnulf had him brought to his lodging; learning the man had never been baptized — 'no one would grant that grace to me, wretched and cast off by the people' — the bishop baptized him: 'Modo, dilecte frater, crede in Dominum Iesum Christum, et continuo recipies corporis animaeque medelam' — 'Now, beloved brother, believe in the Lord Jesus Christ, and you will straightway receive the healing of body and soul.' Then the Vita's strange, beautiful line: 'Fit illico gaudium magnum supernarum virtutum in coelo, SED INGENS LUCTUS PAUPERUM CHRISTI ATQUE MONACHORUM IN SAECULO' — 'There was great joy among the heavenly powers in heaven, but IMMENSE GRIEF among Christ's poor and the monks in the world' — the poor mourning the deathbed baptism that would take their benefactor from them. [The miracle of the mute boy of thirteen at Arnulf's cell window, the demoniac at St Medard, the possessed girl at the Basilica of the Holy Cross — all in the same chapters.]

**DODA, the honest verdict [B — WikiTree's research note, the best modern flag]:** 'The origins and parents of Doda are UNKNOWN... The little information (or better clues) we have suggest that this couple is linked wrongly and possibly due to A CONFLATION AFTER A PREVIOUS MERGE.' The 'Old Saxony' origin is 'considered unlikely or anachronistic by modern scholars'; the Heristal/Savoy traditions are 'speculative'; the Clothilde name-variant 'appears to stem from medieval hagiographies and family trees, where she is sometimes conflated with other noblewomen.' Her names in the wild: Dode, Doda, Oda, Clothilde, Doda von Suevian, DE HERISTAL, van de Schelde — the variant-storm itself evidence of the conflation. Carried: her EXISTENCE and MARRIAGE are solid (the Vita tradition, the sons); her ORIGIN is a blank with legends attached.

**THE FORGERY, DATED MORE PRECISELY [A — Fray thesis, hal.science 2011]:** the senatorial genealogy of Arnulf is 'CERTES UN FAUX, MAIS FORT ANCIEN' — a forgery but an OLD one — 'composed at METZ IN THE MIDDLE OF THE NINTH CENTURY' (not c. 800 — the dating refined). And the finding that matters: the mid-9th-century forgery MENTIONS BOTH AN ARNAUD AND A DODA — 'the first would have been Arnulf's father, the second the COUSIN-GERMAN of Arnaud, both supposedly of senatorial origin, living at the turn of the 6th and 7th centuries.' THE LATER DODA TRADITIONS MAY DERIVE FROM THE FORGER'S OWN ONOMASTIC MATERIAL — the falsifier worked from real names of the Metz aristocracy, and the 'Doda' he inserted may be the ancestor of every later claim about her. The register carries the forgery as the SOURCE of the tradition, not merely a false claim.

**ARNOALD, the full debate [A — Wikipedia's Arnoald article]:** Arnoald (c. 560 – c. 611), Bishop of Metz 601–611, successor of Agilulf, also styled Margrave of the Schelde; son of Ansbertus the senator and Bithilde. The three proposed solutions — Arnoald as father of SAINT ITTA, of DODE, or of ARNULF himself — 'a subject of much debate among genealogists.' Settipani's published evolution: early work made Arnulf Arnoald's son 'a perfect candidate'; later work states Arnulf could NOT be Arnoald's son (no royal blood in the Vita) and instead makes Arnoald Arnulf's FATHER-IN-LAW; 'further speculation indicates Arnulf's father could be a Bodegisel, based on documents from old Frankish legends. THIS STATEMENT IS ALSO UNCERTAIN.'

**THE ŠENK ARTICLE'S FRAME [A — Charles University, 'Political influence on late Merovingian episcopal...']:** the Vita read as a POLITICAL document — the acclamation of Arnulf's election, the Thuringian journey, the interpretation of the Noddo episode — the early seventh-century church politics of Metz laid over the saint's life.

## SAINT 1 — SAINT ARNULF OF METZ: THE HIDDEN, FOUND (3 November 2026 — the murder and the father)

**THE MURDER OF ANSEGISEL — SOLVED at the hypothesis level [S/A — Hummer, Politics and Power in Early Medieval Europe (Cambridge UP 2006); the Vita Beggae (11th c.) via the French Wikipedia's full account]:**
- THE VITA BEGGAE tells it: Ansegisel was assassinated at CHÈVREMONT, near Liège, by an Austrasian noble named GODIN or GUNDOEN — 'WHOM HE HAD PREVIOUSLY RAISED AS HIS OWN SON' (qu'il aurait auparavant élevé comme son fils). THE FOSTER-SON KILLED THE BENEFACTOR — the hidden, found: the name without a story now has one.
- The identification: the killer may be the GUNDOIN who became DUKE IN AUSTRASIA in 669 — Hummer: 'this Duke Gundoin probably is the same Gundoin who, according to later testimony, murdered Pippin of Herstal's father Ansegisel.' Date therefore c. 662–669 (after Childebert the Adopted's death removed the killer's protection).
- THE MOTIVE, per SETTIPANI: VENGEANCE of the family of OTTON — mayor of the palace of Austrasia, predecessor and enemy of Grimoald, killed in 643 — the Wolfoald-Gundoins against the Pippinids/Arnulfings, 'a century of implacable conflict.' HUMMER'S CAUTION, kept verbatim: 'While it is reasonable to assume that the two powerful families would have contended frequently for honores, we should nonetheless avoid the temptation to reify such competition into rigid opposition groups. If we subscribe to blood feuds, we can imagine a century of implacable conflict between the Pippinids and the Wolfoald-Gundoins.'
- Carried: murder at Chèvremont [Vita Beggae, 11th c. — B]; foster-son detail [same grade]; killer = the 669 duke Gundoin [Hummer — probable, flagged]; Otton-vengeance motive [Settipani — hypothesis, flagged]; the hunting-accident variant stays logged alongside.

**THE FATHER — THE VITA GUNDULPHI IN FULL [S — the MGH-bibliothek study, with the Latin]:**
- The Vita Gundulphi says: GUNDULF was the son of MUNDERICH (whom King Theuderic I had killed); he was 'MAGNUS IN AUSTRIA,' and was RAISED IN THE PALACE OF KING CHLOTHAR I TOGETHER WITH HIS BROTHER BODEGISEL, THE DUX. In his old age he addressed 'ARNULF, THE SON OF BODEGISEL AND HIS NEPHEW': 'Hear me, beloved nephew: the punishment of the Lord began when he allowed Munderich to be slain with the sword, the son of Chideric the Parricide! Let us therefore pray that Christ preserve our heads from the coming wrath. For the Almighty has said: "I will visit your sins unto the third and fourth generation."' — ARnulf the FOURTH generation; his uncle Gundulf and his late father Bodegisel the third; Munderich the second; Chideric the Parricide first. THE FAMILY'S OWN TRADITION MADE ARnULF THE GREAT-GRANDSON OF A REGICIDE, DOOMED TO THE THIRD-AND-FOURTH-GENERATION CURSE.
- The candidate: BODEGISEL DUX, who died in 585 'plenus dierum' — Gregory of Tours: 'Obiit his diebus Bodygisilus dux plenus dierum, sed nihil de facultate eius filiis minuatum est' (died full of days, and nothing of his estate was lost to his sons); Venantius Fortunatus dedicated poems to Bodegisel AND TO HIS WIFE PALATINA (daughter of Bishop GALLOMAGNUS of Troyes). IF this is Arnulf's father, THEN ARnULF WAS THE SON OF A FRANKISH-ROMAN MIXED MARRIAGE — which would explain the whole puzzle: 'Francorum' from the father's line, the senatorial aroma from the mother. Gregory's odd stress that the sons inherited fully is weighed (political, not legal, reasons suspected).
- THE SCHOLARLY SPLIT, both halves kept: SETTIPANI — Arnulf's father = the Bodegisel MURDERED BY THE MOB at Carthage (Chrodoara's husband, d. 588/9); THE NIBELUNG AUTHOR (FMG Foundations) — accepts 'Arnulf's father, Bodogisel, as the son of St. Gundulf,' arguing the mob-murder would have been too dramatic for the Vita Gundulphi to omit: 'If that Bodogisel was actually Saint Arnulf's father, he is not attested elsewhere, but our sources are so scanty that that seems entirely natural.' BOTH agree on the Vita Gundulphi's value and the Cologne kinglets derivation; they differ on which Bodegisel. The register carries both, weighed equally, named.
- The Fontanelles pedigrees: Settipani's re-reading (commemoration lists misread as pedigrees); the sequence 'Haldgislus, Momulinus, Batgislus, Aodulf (error for Arnulf), Ansghislus' logged.

**SIDE FINDS:** (1) The Clotilda=Doda claim gains the TOMB detail [A — Hungarian Wikipedia]: King Theuderic III's grave inscription names DODA as the queen, presumably the same woman as Clotilda. (2) Pepin of Herstal's rise documented: BATTLE OF TERTRY, June 687; mayor of Austrasia from 688. (3) THE SAME-NAME TRAP, second instance: the search's Gundulf hits were dominated by GUNDULF OF ROCHESTER (1024–1108, builder of the White Tower, 'father of the Royal Engineers') — a millennium later; the family's Gundulf is the 6th-c. courtier of Provence, carried apart.

**VERDICT ON "THE HIDDEN":** FOUND — the foster-son murder at Chèvremont, the 669 duke, the Otton-vengeance hypothesis; AND the father's family made the great-grandson of a regicide under a biblical curse. Both at their grades, both named.

## SAINT 1 — SAINT ARNULF OF METZ: THE 634 WILL, IN FULL (3 November 2026 — the oldest document in the whole register)

**THE WILL OF ADALGISEL-GRIMO, COMPLETE [S — the Oxford Cult of Saints database entry (dlambert/Levison 1932); the manuscript: a 10th-c. copy, Landeshauptarchiv Koblenz, Bestand 1A, Nr 1]:**
- Date: 30 December 634 ('the 3rd day before the Kalends of January in the 12th year of King Dagobert'). Testator: ADALGISELUS, also called GRIMO, DEACON of the church of Verdun. Witnesses: PAUL, Bishop of Verdun; four presbyters and deacons; TWO LAYMEN.
- THE BEQUESTS, in the will's own order: (1) his MONASTERY AND XENODOCHIUM at LONGUYON, dedicated to ST AGATHA — a home for SIXTEEN CHOSEN POOR, the abbot charged to keep them supplied 'with food, clothing and shelter' and always the same number — the most favored institution: several villas, houses, vineyards; (2) the CHURCH OF ST PETER AT TEMMELS — estates, a MILL, and flocks of sheep WITH THEIR SHEPHERDS; (3) the church of St Peter and St VITONUS at Verdun, 'WHERE THE LEPERS LIVE' — an estate; (4) the LOCA SANCTORUM he built at THOLEY — left with all its land to the church of Verdun, and because the Bishop of Trier had consecrated them, he is to keep the right of baptism there, for which Verdun pays him 31 GOLD PIECES a year for the chrism; (5) 600 solidi to the poor of ST MARTIN OF TOURS, 'where he rests in the body'; (6) the vineyards he held IN USUFRUCT from ST MAXIMINUS OF TRIER and from the church of ST GEORGE AT AMAY — 'where my aunt is buried' — to return to those churches at his death, and to St Maximinus an estate's portion and TWO COWHERDS WITH THEIR COWS; (7) further gifts: the church of Verdun; the church at HUY; THE LEPERS OF METZ; THE LEPERS OF MAASTRICHT.
- WHY IT MATTERS BEYOND THE FAMILY: one of the earliest western attestations of the cult of ST GEORGE (the Amay church); the richest surviving window onto 7th-century Austrasian piety, property, and poor-relief; 'one of the few' Merovingian testaments preserved at all.

**THE FAMILY, from the will and the dissertations [A — Jaouen 2024 thesis]:** the will names his NEPHEW THE DUX BOBO; his AUNT buried at St-George's, Amay (= CHRODOARA); his SISTER THE DEACONESS ERMENGUNDIS; and cousins or nephews, sons of Ado. The nephew-duke 'Bobo' rides on the same name-web as Bodegisel-Bobo — logged, not merged.

**CHRODOARA = SAINTE ODE, confirmed [A — the Hesbaye study]:** 'Chrodoara and Sainte Ode (Oda), venerated from the 11th century, would be the same person. The canons had realized that the name Chrodoara, become unusual and barbarous, would no longer be perceived by the population: they had substituted the easier appellation of SANCTA ODA.' Her five St-George foundations: Amay, two at Huy (Rioul and Les Prés), Waleffes, Warfusée. The sarcophagus: 'the most spectacular Merovingian work of art preserved in Belgium'; the excavations: twenty Merovingian tombs found in 1915 (Dr Wibin), 268 more in the Walloon Region's 2007–2009 digs. Her church kept her reliquary until the Revolution.

**THE METZ GESTA, and the ring [A — the Hari thesis on the Gesta Episcoporum Messins]:** the LIBER DE EPISCOPIS METTENSIBUS — the book CHARLEMAGNE HAD COMMISSIONED ('Haec ego... a praecelso rege Karolo referente cognovi' — 'these things I learned from the most excellent King Charles himself, the assertor of the truth') — carries the inheritance scene: CHLODULF uncharitable (refuses his inheritance share as alms to the poor); ANSEGISEL accepts and receives 'the blessing of the saint for himself and his descendants.' And THE RING: 'The testimony of Charlemagne on his ancestor's ring shows the reader that the king himself considered the bishop of Metz his MOST IMPORTANT ANCESTOR.'
